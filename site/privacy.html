EchoScribe

Privacy policy

In force from 5 October 2026.

EchoScribe is a Windows application that turns your speech into typed text. This policy says what data it handles, where that data goes, and what it never does. It is written to be read, not to cover us.

The short version

  • Your transcribed words are stored on your own computer and nowhere else.
  • Your voice goes to Deepgram, using your own Deepgram account, and is never saved by EchoScribe.
  • Sign-in is handled by Clerk. EchoScribe never sees or stores a password.
  • There is no EchoScribe server. EchoScribe collects no analytics, no crash reports, and shows no advertising.
  • This website has one form, for the download link. The email address you enter there goes to Kit, our mailing list service.

What EchoScribe stores on your computer

Everything EchoScribe keeps lives in one database file in your Windows user profile, in the application data folder for EchoScribe. It holds:

  • the text of your past dictations (your history), each tied to your account;
  • the custom words you have taught it;
  • your chosen transcription language and your settings;
  • the email address and display name of your account, as given by your sign-in provider.

Your Deepgram API key and your sign-in session are kept in Windows Credential Manager, the operating system's own store for secrets. The key is never shown in full again once it is saved, and it is never written to any log or file.

Uninstalling EchoScribe and deleting that application data folder removes all of this. Signing out removes the session from Credential Manager and leaves your history on the machine, tied to the account that made it.

Your voice and Deepgram

When you press the dictation hotkey, EchoScribe opens your microphone, and something visible on screen says so. It never listens without that. The audio is streamed to Deepgram, a speech recognition service, using the API key from your own Deepgram account. The text comes back and is typed at your cursor and kept in your local history. The audio itself is never written to disk, never logged, and never kept after the text comes back.

Because the key is yours, Deepgram's handling of your audio is governed by your own agreement with Deepgram and by Deepgram's privacy policy, not by EchoScribe. EchoScribe has no account of its own with Deepgram and no access to what Deepgram keeps.

Signing in, and what we hold about you

EchoScribe uses Clerk, a sign-in service, so that your words and settings belong to an account and stay signed in across restarts. Sign-in happens in your browser on Clerk's pages, never inside EchoScribe. You can sign in with a one-time code sent to your email, or with your Google account.

The account record Clerk holds for you is your email address, your name, and, if you signed in with Google, the profile picture Google supplies. EchoScribe itself keeps only the email address and name, on your computer, to show you who is signed in. Clerk's handling of that record is governed by Clerk's privacy policy.

If you sign in with Google, EchoScribe requests only your basic profile: your name, your email address and your profile picture. That information is used solely to create and identify your EchoScribe account and to show your name in the app. It is not used for advertising, not sold, and not shared with anyone other than Clerk, which performs the sign-in. EchoScribe's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What EchoScribe does not do

  • It runs no server of its own. Nothing you say or type is routed through EchoScribe's infrastructure, because there is none.
  • It collects no analytics, no usage statistics and no crash reports.
  • It never opens the microphone without a visible sign on screen. There is no always-on listening and no wake word.
  • It never types into a password field, even while you are dictating.
  • It never sends your transcribed text anywhere except to the cursor you dictated into and to your local history.

The download form on this site

To get the installer you enter your email address on this website. That address goes to Kit, the mailing list service we use, which sends you one email with the download link and keeps your address on our list so we can tell you what changes in EchoScribe. Every email we send has an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing removes you from the list. This is the only form on this website, and the app itself never sees that list and never reports to it. Kit's handling of your address is governed by Kit's privacy policy.

Deleting your account

To delete the account record held by Clerk, email hello@echoscribe.exentrik.co from the address on the account and we will remove it. Your local history is yours to delete by removing EchoScribe's application data folder.

Changes to this policy

If EchoScribe ever starts collecting something new, this page will say so before it does, with the date the change takes effect.
